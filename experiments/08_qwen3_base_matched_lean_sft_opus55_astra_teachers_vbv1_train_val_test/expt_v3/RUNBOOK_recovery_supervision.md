## Executable and state

Host runtime root: `/lfs/skampere1/0/brando9/rl4cv_e08/sft_v3`. The installed cron entries run `/usr/bin/python3 <this experiment>/recovery_watch.py --root <runtime root>` every five minutes and at reboot. They are tagged `rl4cv-e08-v3-recovery`. Cron and its files survive owner exit; reboot recovery requires the host and mounted filesystems to return. The next periodic tick retries after an early boot mount failure.

`recovery_config.json` contains the exact fixed launch arrays and finite inventory; `recovery_state.json` is the observation and attempt record; `recovery_watch.log` is the cron log. The watch uses `recovery.lock` while checking and reserving a launch. `owner_ack.json` records the actual owner process (boot identifier, process start ticks and process identifier), model, effort, permissions, inputs and training evidence. A recovery process additionally writes `recovery_owner_process.json` before launching its command-line client, preventing a duplicate while a new acknowledgement is pending. Every native recovery output and exit code is retained by attempt.

The watch observes both phase schedulers, child liveness, scheduler heartbeat, work-log/output freshness, analysis errors, failures, free bytes and completion evidence. A stale live process is reported for investigation; it is not killed. Legacy Phase A jobs record only process identifiers, so the watch explicitly reports their weaker identity evidence and never silently restarts them. The replacement owner must reconcile identities and adopt healthy children before restarting any scheduler. Process identifier reuse and zombies are handled in owner fencing.

An owner may intentionally transfer its writer role by writing `owner_handoff.json` with its exact `owner` and `attempt` from the acknowledgement and `released: true`. It must stop repository/runtime mutations at that point. The old owner may then observe and verify the successor without becoming a second writer. No training child is terminated by this protocol.

## Explicit external resource wait

The owner stays active until all independent work is complete. Only then may it arm `external_resource_wait.json` and release through `owner_handoff.json` with `mode: EXTERNAL_RESOURCE_WAIT`. This guard is **not armed during recovery-01's active Phase A work**. Ordinary remote controllers continue polling and admitting the frozen layouts; the guard only avoids spending a new model-owner attempt while capacity is unchanged.

Write both records atomically using `recovery_watch.write`. The wait requires `schema: 1`, `status: EXTERNAL_RESOURCE_WAIT`, exact current `owner` and `attempt`, `recorded_unix`, the unchanged `deadline_unix`, a concrete `reason`, `independent_work_complete: true`, and `admissions_absent_at_recording: true`. Save `phase_snapshot: recovery_watch.resource_snapshot(runtime_root)` after confirming all available scoring, diagnostics, export verification and publication have finished. `expected_remote_admissions` contains only the absent shared-runtime basenames for blocked 8B/14B training or generation, such as `remote_E_14b.json`; check their absence immediately before recording. Never arm while an admitted workload or an available independent task still needs supervision.

The handoff must repeat the acknowledgement's exact identity and attempt and set `released: true`. After release, stop writing. The watch waits for a still-live released owner to exit, and always fences a live recovery runner. It requires the latest recorded recovery attempt to match, and retains the original deadline and total/candidate attempt caps.

An unchanged wait records `DEFERRED_EXTERNAL_RESOURCE` without a new owner or attempt. An actual remote admission record, changed execution status/identity, failed or dead child, dead/stale unfinished scheduler, or analysis error wakes ordinary bounded recovery. Remote process identities are never checked against local `/proc`. A corrupt expected admission record wakes inspection; invalid optional wait metadata falls back to ordinary recovery. Completed phases do not produce stale-heartbeat faults, and only expected lack of scientific progress during capacity wait is deferrable.

An optional ordinary-code route observer may publish `external_capacity_event.json` with an increasing `sequence`, a `route` object containing verified `eligible: true`, and `fingerprint = sha256(json.dumps(route, sort_keys=True, separators=(',', ':')).encode())`. The wait's `capacity_baseline` records its earlier sequence/fingerprint. Only a newer, recomputed, different eligible route wakes the owner; timestamps belong outside `route`. This optional producer is not installed by the guard. Direct prepared-host recovery already has a real event producer: the existing remote admission controllers.

[The resource-wait self-test](results/resource_wait_selftest.json) confirms a real harmless tmux recovery child runs after a synthetic admission, preserves a separate healthy child, retains native exit evidence, and does not launch twice.
